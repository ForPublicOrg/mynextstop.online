Rangdum season by season

Winter December to February Do not go

Dec-Feb the Pensi La and the Kargil road are both snowed shut, the camps are folded and only the monks stay on. Not reachable.

Summer March to May Do not go

Mar-May the upper Suru road past Parkachik stays under snow and the Rangdum camps are shut; the Kargil to Padum run usually reopens at May end.

Monsoon June to September Prime time

June brings the first jeeps through; Jul-Sep is the window, marmots on a green 4,000 m plain and the Drang Drung glacier an hour on at Pensi La.

Autumn October to November Shoulder

Early Oct is clear, empty and bitterly cold at 4,033 m; by November the camps have gone and the pass shuts again.

What Rangdum is actually like

Two hamlets, a tourist bungalow and canvas camps that fold in October; evenings are cold, starry and silent bar the wind.

Getting to Rangdum

Kargil to Padum shared taxi or the twice-weekly bus, about 6h to Rangdum via Panikhar and the Suru valley.

Common questions

When is the best time to visit Rangdum?

July to September is peak season in Rangdum. June and October is shoulder season: thinner crowds, a bit more risk in the weather. Avoid November to May. June brings the first jeeps through; Jul-Sep is the window, marmots on a green 4,000 m plain and the Drang Drung glacier an hour on at Pensi La.

How many days do you need in Rangdum?

2 days is the usual stay. Two hamlets, a tourist bungalow and canvas camps that fold in October; evenings are cold, starry and silent bar the wind.

How do you get to Rangdum?

Kargil to Padum shared taxi or the twice-weekly bus, about 6h to Rangdum via Panikhar and the Suru valley. Practical ways in: road.

Is Rangdum expensive, and does it get crowded?

Shoestring by Indian travel standards, and it runs quiet at the busiest time of year. Doable solo with some planning.
