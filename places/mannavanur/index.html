Mannavanur season by season

Winter December to February Prime time

Nights drop near 5C and frost sits on the grass; February is the pick, clear blue days for the lake loop and the road out to Poombarai.

Summer March to May Prime time

March and April hold 22C days and flowering shola edges; from mid May storms build most afternoons, so do the walking before lunch.

Monsoon June to September Prime time

June and July are wind, drizzle and whiteout fog on the plateau; September clears quickly and the grassland is at its greenest of the year.

Autumn October to November Quiet, but open

The northeast monsoon does its worst here: October and November bring long rain, leeches and slips on the Kodai ghat road, so come later.

Common questions

When is the best time to visit Mannavanur?

February to April and September is peak season in Mannavanur. May to June and December to January is shoulder season: thinner crowds, a bit more risk in the weather. No month is a write-off here. Nights drop near 5C and frost sits on the grass; February is the pick, clear blue days for the lake loop and the road out to Poombarai.

How many days do you need in Mannavanur?

3 days is the usual stay. Farm homestays with wood fires and dinner at 8, a few Kodai day trippers by the lake, then only sheep, wind and stars.

How do you get to Mannavanur?

Kodaikanal is 35 km and 1h30 by bus or share taxi; the railhead is Kodai Road, 4h down the ghat. Practical ways in: train and road.

Is Mannavanur expensive, and does it get crowded?

Mid-range by Indian travel standards, and it runs quiet at the busiest time of year. Fine solo.
