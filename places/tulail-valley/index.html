Tulail Valley (Badugam) season by season

Winter December to February Do not go

Dec-Feb Razdan Pass is shut and Tulail lives off stored rice and firewood; nothing moves in or out but the army, so it is not a trip.

Summer March to May Shoulder

March and April the pass is still buried. It usually opens late May, when the Kishanganga is loud with melt and the barley goes in.

Monsoon June to September Prime time

Jun-Sep is the window: warm days, cold nights, potato and rajma fields, and the rough 40 km run east of Dawar to Badugam and Chakwali.

Autumn October to November Shoulder

October turns the poplars gold and empties the road; once November snow lands the pass closes and Tulail is on its own until spring.

What Tulail Valley (Badugam) is actually like

Log-and-stone houses, a few new homestays and no hotels; evenings are noon chai and the last shop shutting with the light.

Getting to Tulail Valley (Badugam)

Srinagar to Bandipora, shared sumo over Razdan Pass to Dawar (5h), then 40 km further east up the valley to Badugam.

Common questions

When is the best time to visit Tulail Valley (Badugam)?

June to September is peak season in Tulail Valley (Badugam). May and October is shoulder season: thinner crowds, a bit more risk in the weather. Avoid November to April. Jun-Sep is the window: warm days, cold nights, potato and rajma fields, and the rough 40 km run east of Dawar to Badugam and Chakwali.

How many days do you need in Tulail Valley (Badugam)?

3 days is the usual stay. Log-and-stone houses, a few new homestays and no hotels; evenings are noon chai and the last shop shutting with the light.

How do you get to Tulail Valley (Badugam)?

Srinagar to Bandipora, shared sumo over Razdan Pass to Dawar (5h), then 40 km further east up the valley to Badugam. Practical ways in: road.

Is Tulail Valley (Badugam) expensive, and does it get crowded?

Shoestring by Indian travel standards, and it runs quiet at the busiest time of year. Doable solo with some planning.
