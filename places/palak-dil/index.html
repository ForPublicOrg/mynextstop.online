Palak Dil (Phura) season by season

Winter December to February Prime time

Nov-Feb the 6 km trail from Phura is dry and leech-free, the lake sits glassy under mist till 9 am and winter ducks join the 200-plus bird list; nights a mild 10-12 C.

Summer March to May Shoulder

March and April stay walkable but hot and hazy, 32 C by noon with jhum smoke over the hills; by May thunderstorms hit most afternoons and the lake path turns to mud.

Monsoon June to September Shoulder

Jun-Aug southern Mizoram takes 3,000 mm of rain: the Siaha-Phura road slips, sumos stop for days and the trail is a leech bath. September eases off and the lake is brim full.

Autumn October to November Prime time

October clears with the forest at its greenest and the lake at its widest; November is the sweet spot, dry trails, clear skies, and most days the whole shore is yours.

What Palak Dil (Phura) is actually like

A Mara village of tin roofs and a church bell, a government tourist lodge and a bamboo forest lodge; evenings are early and quiet

Common questions

When is the best time to visit Palak Dil (Phura)?

November to February is peak season in Palak Dil (Phura). March to April and September to October is shoulder season: thinner crowds, a bit more risk in the weather. Avoid June to August. October clears with the forest at its greenest and the lake at its widest; November is the sweet spot, dry trails, clear skies, and most days the whole shore is yours.

How many days do you need in Palak Dil (Phura)?

2 days is the usual stay. A Mara village of tin roofs and a church bell, a government tourist lodge and a bamboo forest lodge; evenings are early and quiet.

How do you get to Palak Dil (Phura)?

Night sumo Aizawl to Siaha, 378 km (12 h), then a daily sumo to Phura (3 h); the lake is a 6 km forest walk from there. Practical ways in: road.

Is Palak Dil (Phura) expensive, and does it get crowded?

Shoestring by Indian travel standards, and it runs quiet at the busiest time of year. Doable solo with some planning.
