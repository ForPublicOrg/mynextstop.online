Laya season by season

Winter December to February Do not go

Avoid Dec to Feb: deep cold at about 3,800 m, snow on the climb from the road end and few services in the village.

Summer March to May Prime time

Apr and May are the best: warming days, rhododendrons along the trail and yaks on fresh pasture around the village.

Monsoon June to September Shoulder

Jul and Aug are wet, with leeches and slips on the Mo Chhu trail; Jun and Sep are greener and manageable with a guide.

Autumn October to November Prime time

Oct is the highlight, with the Royal Highland Festival and clear peaks; by late Nov the nights are bitterly cold.

Signature festival

Royal Highland Festival (Oct)

What Laya is actually like

Camping or simple farmhouse stays, yak butter tea by the hearth, and Layap women in conical bamboo hats.

Common questions

When is the best time to visit Laya?

April to May and October is peak season in Laya. March, June, September and November is shoulder season: thinner crowds, a bit more risk in the weather. Avoid July to August and December to February. Apr and May are the best: warming days, rhododendrons along the trail and yaks on fresh pasture around the village.

How many days do you need in Laya?

4 days is the usual stay. Camping or simple farmhouse stays, yak butter tea by the hearth, and Layap women in conical bamboo hats.

How do you get to Laya?

Punakha to Gasa is about 80 km, 3 h; a rough road runs on to Taktsimakha, then a 2 to 3 h climb. Route permit and guide. Practical ways in: road.

Is Laya expensive, and does it get crowded?

Splurge by Indian travel standards, and it runs quiet at the busiest time of year. Doable solo with some planning.

Is there a festival in Laya?

Royal Highland Festival (Oct). Time a trip around it and you see the place at its loudest, but book beds early.

What are the best things to do in Laya?

Koina, Gasa Tsachu, Gasa Dzong and Mo Chhu. Old trek camp in the Mo Chhu gorge below Laya on the Gasa route, set in thick conifer forest beside the river.
