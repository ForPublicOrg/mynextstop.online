Kudremukh season by season

Winter December to February Prime time

Dec-Jan is prime trekking: dry trail, cold Mullodi mornings, and the 1,894 m summit line clear before the haze builds.

Summer March to May Shoulder

March still works at a push; Apr-May the shola grass goes brown, streams shrink and heat haze flattens the famous ridge views.

Monsoon June to September Shoulder

Jun-Aug the forest office often suspends permits: swollen stream crossings, relentless leeches, zero-view mist; Sept reopens green.

Autumn October to November Prime time

Oct-Nov is the classic window: meadows at their greenest, trailside streams still alive and washed post-rain skies.

What Kudremukh is actually like

Mullodi homestays feed trekkers at one long table; weekend groups from Bengaluru, near silence midweek.

Things to do in Kudremukh

The local places worth your time in and around Kudremukh, with how far each sits from the centre. Tap a name to open it in Google Maps.

  • Hanuman Gundi Falls

    Waterfall · 20 km out

    Forest fall off the Karkala road inside the park, roughly 400 steps down to the pool; ticket at the gate, shut in heavy monsoon.

  • Kadambi Falls

    Waterfall · 12 km out

    Roadside fall on the Kalasa to Karkala highway, seen from the bend with no walk; full in monsoon, a trickle by February.

  • Lakya Dam

    Reservoir · 10 km out

    Tailings dam left by the closed iron ore mine, still water under bare red slopes; you pass a forest check post, nothing to buy there.

  • Kalaseshwara Temple

    Temple · 18 km out

    Old Shiva temple above the Bhadra at Kalasa, the town every trek here starts from; tirthas on the rock below, open dawn to dusk.

  • Horanadu Annapoorneshwari Temple

    Temple · 26 km out

    Temple that feeds every visitor free at noon and night; queues run long on weekends, shoulders and knees covered inside.

  • Kurinjal Peak

    Trek · 14 km out

    Grass ridge trek from Bhagavathi Nature Camp, about 5 hours return; online permit only, a guide is compulsory, start by 7 am.

Common questions

When is the best time to visit Kudremukh?

October to January is peak season in Kudremukh. February to March and September is shoulder season: thinner crowds, a bit more risk in the weather. Avoid June to August. Oct-Nov is the classic window: meadows at their greenest, trailside streams still alive and washed post-rain skies.

How many days do you need in Kudremukh?

2 days is the usual stay. Mullodi homestays feed trekkers at one long table; weekend groups from Bengaluru, near silence midweek.

How do you get to Kudremukh?

Overnight bus Bengaluru to Kalasa, then jeep up the rough track to Mullodi; trek permits are online-only now. Practical ways in: road.

Is Kudremukh expensive, and does it get crowded?

Shoestring by Indian travel standards, and it runs steady at the busiest time of year. Fine solo.

What are the best things to do in Kudremukh?

Hanuman Gundi Falls, Kadambi Falls, Lakya Dam, Kalaseshwara Temple, Horanadu Annapoorneshwari Temple and Kurinjal Peak. Forest fall off the Karkala road inside the park, roughly 400 steps down to the pool; ticket at the gate, shut in heavy monsoon.
