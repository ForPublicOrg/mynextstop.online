Penukonda season by season

Winter December to February Prime time

Nov-Feb gives 28 C days and cold mornings, the right window for the two hour climb past Gagan Mahal to the Jain temple on the hill.

Summer March to May Shoulder

Mar-Apr work if you start at 6 am. By May the bare granite radiates past 40 C and there is no water anywhere on the way up.

Monsoon June to September Shoulder

Jun-Sep brings only thin Rayalaseema rain, enough to green the boulder fields; showers are short and the fort path stays walkable.

Autumn October to November Prime time

Oct-Nov is clear and quiet: the haze lifts, you see the Lepakshi plain from the top, and the town's ruins have nobody in them.

What Penukonda is actually like

A working market town with basic lodges and dhabas; the ruins stand inside the lived-in streets, so evenings are shutters and idli carts

Common questions

When is the best time to visit Penukonda?

November to February is peak season in Penukonda. March to April and June to October is shoulder season: thinner crowds, a bit more risk in the weather. Avoid May. Oct-Nov is clear and quiet: the haze lifts, you see the Lepakshi plain from the top, and the town's ruins have nobody in them.

How many days do you need in Penukonda?

2 days is the usual stay. A working market town with basic lodges and dhabas; the ruins stand inside the lived-in streets, so evenings are shutters and idli carts.

How do you get to Penukonda?

Penukonda Junction on the Bengaluru-Guntakal line, 3 h from Bengaluru; Kempegowda airport is 90 km up NH44. Practical ways in: flight, train and road.

Is Penukonda expensive, and does it get crowded?

Shoestring by Indian travel standards, and it runs quiet at the busiest time of year. Fine solo.
