Malari (Niti Valley) season by season

Winter December to February Do not go

Dec to Mar the families move down to Ghat and Joshimath, the stone houses are padlocked and the road above Tapovan closes with snow.

Summer March to May Prime time

May and June are the season: 8 to 20 C, barley in the terraces, Bhotia families back home and the Dhauli valley road open to Malari.

Monsoon June to September Prime time

June stays clear. July and August bring the worst slides on the Joshimath to Malari road and army convoys go first; September clears.

Autumn October to November Prime time

October is the best month, cold nights, gold willows and hard blue sky over the Niti ridge; by mid November the village empties again.

Common questions

When is the best time to visit Malari (Niti Valley)?

May to June and September to October is peak season in Malari (Niti Valley). April and November is shoulder season: thinner crowds, a bit more risk in the weather. Avoid December to March. May and June are the season: 8 to 20 C, barley in the terraces, Bhotia families back home and the Dhauli valley road open to Malari.

How many days do you need in Malari (Niti Valley)?

3 days is the usual stay. A dozen homestays and a rest house among stone and slate houses; army trucks, shepherds and long silent evenings.

How do you get to Malari (Niti Valley)?

Joshimath to Malari 62 km by shared jeep, about 3 h; carry ID, the checkpost logs everyone past Tapovan. Practical ways in: road.

Is Malari (Niti Valley) expensive, and does it get crowded?

Shoestring by Indian travel standards, and it runs quiet at the busiest time of year. Doable solo with some planning.
