Walong (Dong & Kibithoo) season by season

Winter December to February Prime time

Dec-Feb are clear and cold, 2-3 C nights and dry days; the pre-dawn climb above Dong for India's first sunrise is the reason to be here, and the road stays open

Summer March to May Prime time

Mar-May bring rhododendron on the Kibithoo ridges and warm days by the Lohit; hazy afternoons and short storms by May, but the Tezu Sumos still run daily

Monsoon June to September Do not go

Jun-Sep the Lohit is in flood and the Hayuliang-Walong road is cut by landslides for days; Anjaw is effectively closed to travellers. Not now

Autumn October to November Prime time

Oct dries slowly and by November the valley is at its best: hot spring by the river, war memorial, Kaho and Kibithoo homestays and a road with no other tourists

What Walong (Dong & Kibithoo) is actually like

An army town at heart: an inspection bungalow, a few homestays, Mishmi villages and soldiers who tell the 1962 story over tea

Getting to Walong (Dong & Kibithoo)

Common questions

When is the best time to visit Walong (Dong & Kibithoo)?

November to March is peak season in Walong (Dong & Kibithoo). April to May and October is shoulder season: thinner crowds, a bit more risk in the weather. Avoid June to September. Dec-Feb are clear and cold, 2-3 C nights and dry days; the pre-dawn climb above Dong for India's first sunrise is the reason to be here, and the road stays open.

How many days do you need in Walong (Dong & Kibithoo)?

3 days is the usual stay. An army town at heart: an inspection bungalow, a few homestays, Mishmi villages and soldiers who tell the 1962 story over tea.

How do you get to Walong (Dong & Kibithoo)?

Tezu to Walong by shared Sumo, 200 km and 9-11 h via Hayuliang; Tezu is 3 h from Tinsukia rail or Dibrugarh air. ILP needed. Practical ways in: road.

Is Walong (Dong & Kibithoo) expensive, and does it get crowded?

Shoestring by Indian travel standards, and it runs quiet at the busiest time of year. Doable solo with some planning.
