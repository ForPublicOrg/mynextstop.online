Bateshwar & Chambal Safari (Bah) season by season

Winter December to February Prime time

Peak: gharial stacked on cold sandbars, skimmers and winter waterfowl in, mist over the ravines; boats need booking ahead.

Summer March to May Shoulder

March-April safaris still run in rising heat; by May the Chambal belt is at 46 °C and boat rides become a punishment.

Monsoon June to September Do not go

Jul-Sep the river runs in spate through the ravines, boats stop and the sanctuary is effectively off limits until October.

Autumn October to November Prime time

Season restarts in October on clearing water; November adds the month-long Bateshwar cattle fair on the Yamuna sands.

Signature festival

Nov: Bateshwar cattle fair (peaks at Kartik Purnima)

Common questions

When is the best time to visit Bateshwar & Chambal Safari (Bah)?

November to February is peak season in Bateshwar & Chambal Safari (Bah). March to April and October is shoulder season: thinner crowds, a bit more risk in the weather. Avoid May to September. Season restarts in October on clearing water; November adds the month-long Bateshwar cattle fair on the Yamuna sands.

How many days do you need in Bateshwar & Chambal Safari (Bah)?

2 days is the usual stay. Mela Kothi lodge under old tamarinds, birders and photographers at dinner; no backpacker scene, boats booked ahead.

How do you get to Bateshwar & Chambal Safari (Bah)?

Agra 70 km, about 2 h by road via Bah; lodge arranges boats at Nandgaon ghat, no public transport for that leg. Practical ways in: train and road.

Is Bateshwar & Chambal Safari (Bah) expensive, and does it get crowded?

Splurge by Indian travel standards, and it runs quiet at the busiest time of year. Doable solo with some planning.

Is there a festival in Bateshwar & Chambal Safari (Bah)?

Nov: Bateshwar cattle fair (peaks at Kartik Purnima). Time a trip around it and you see the place at its loudest, but book beds early.
