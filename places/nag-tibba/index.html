Nag Tibba season by season

Winter December to February Prime time

December-March is the point of this trek: knee-deep snow on the ridge, -5C at camp, and Bandarpoonch clear to the north.

Summer March to May Prime time

April-May still works at 10-22C, but haze off the plains flattens the summit view and the water sources start running low.

Monsoon June to September Mixed, read the detail

July-August is a leech-ridden slog with real lightning risk on the open ridge and slides on the Dehradun-Pantwari road.

Autumn October to November Prime time

October-November is the other good window: dry ground, cold nights and a completely uncluttered Gangotri-group skyline.

What Nag Tibba is actually like

Common questions

When is the best time to visit Nag Tibba?

October to March is peak season in Nag Tibba. April to May is shoulder season: thinner crowds, a bit more risk in the weather. Avoid July to August. December-March is the point of this trek: knee-deep snow on the ridge, -5C at camp, and Bandarpoonch clear to the north.

How many days do you need in Nag Tibba?

2 days is the usual stay. The easiest real Himalayan snow trek out of Delhi; weekend groups from Dehradun, homestays and camps at Pantwari.

How do you get to Nag Tibba?

Pantwari trailhead 90 km, 3.5 h from Dehradun by bus or jeep; 4 km climb to base camp, 3 km more to the summit. Practical ways in: road.

Is Nag Tibba expensive, and does it get crowded?

Shoestring by Indian travel standards, and it runs steady at the busiest time of year. Fine solo.
