Khajjiar season by season

Winter December to February Prime time

Dec-Jan can bring snowfall on the meadow, popular for a day trip; otherwise cold, thin on facilities.

Summer March to May Prime time

Apr-May bring the meadow to its greenest, with pony rides and paragliding in full swing.

Monsoon June to September Shoulder

Jun is fine; Jul-Aug turn the meadow to mud and the Dalhousie-Khajjiar road landslide-prone.

Autumn October to November Prime time

Clear skies and cool air make Oct-Nov ideal for photos without summer's day-tripper crowds.

  • Khajji Nag Temple

    Temple · in town

    Twelfth century Nag shrine with a golden dome at the meadow's edge, carved Pandava figures inside; shoes off at the porch

  • Kalatop Wildlife Sanctuary

    Wildlife · 10 km out

    Deodar sanctuary between here and Dalhousie, a level 3 km track to the rest house; buy the ticket at the Lakkarmandi gate

  • Dainkund Peak

    Viewpoint · 13 km out

    2745 m ridge on the Dalhousie road with the Pholani Devi shrine on top; the last stretch is on foot, the gate shuts by evening

Common questions

When is the best time to visit Khajjiar?

April to May, October and December is peak season in Khajjiar. January to March, June, September and November is shoulder season: thinner crowds, a bit more risk in the weather. Avoid July to August. Apr-May bring the meadow to its greenest, with pony rides and paragliding in full swing.

How many days do you need in Khajjiar?

3 days is the usual stay. A day-trip stop for Dalhousie tourists, not really a solo base.

How do you get to Khajjiar?

Reach via Dalhousie (24km, ~45 min taxi); base in Dalhousie. Practical ways in: flight and road.

Is Khajjiar expensive, and does it get crowded?

Mid-range by Indian travel standards, and it runs busy at the busiest time of year. Doable solo with some planning.

What are the best things to do in Khajjiar?

Khajji Nag Temple, Kalatop Wildlife Sanctuary and Dainkund Peak. Twelfth century Nag shrine with a golden dome at the meadow's edge, carved Pandava figures inside; shoes off at the porch.
