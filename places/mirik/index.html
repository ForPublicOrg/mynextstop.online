Mirik season by season

Winter December to February Shoulder

Dec-Feb is 3-13 C with the lake often under mist; quiet and cheap, but Kanchenjunga hides more mornings than it shows.

Summer March to May Prime time

March-April is the pick: 12-22 C, the Sumendu lake loop and tea garden paths; May greys out as pre-monsoon showers build.

Monsoon June to September Do not go

Jun-Sep dumps over 2500 mm; the Mirik-Darjeeling road slips regularly and the lake bowl sits inside cloud for days.

Autumn October to November Prime time

October-November is the best air of the year here, with the orange orchards on the Pashupati road heading to harvest.

What Mirik is actually like

Day-trippers from Siliguri clear out by evening and the lake goes quiet; small hotels, momo shops, no backpackers.

Getting to Mirik

Bagdogra and NJP are both about 2 h by shared jeep; onward jeeps to Darjeeling take another 2 h.

Common questions

When is the best time to visit Mirik?

March to April and October to November is peak season in Mirik. May and December to February is shoulder season: thinner crowds, a bit more risk in the weather. Avoid June to September. October-November is the best air of the year here, with the orange orchards on the Pashupati road heading to harvest.

How many days do you need in Mirik?

2 days is the usual stay. Day-trippers from Siliguri clear out by evening and the lake goes quiet; small hotels, momo shops, no backpackers.

How do you get to Mirik?

Bagdogra and NJP are both about 2 h by shared jeep; onward jeeps to Darjeeling take another 2 h. Practical ways in: flight, train and road.

Is Mirik expensive, and does it get crowded?

Shoestring by Indian travel standards, and it runs steady at the busiest time of year. Fine solo.
