Lakhaniya Dari (Ahraura) season by season

Winter December to February Quiet, but open

Dec-Feb the stream runs low and clear; cool weather suits the rocky walk in, though the main drop is only a ribbon.

Summer March to May Mixed, read the detail

March leaves a trickle; Apr-May the rocks scorch and the falls go dry, so skip the walk until the rains are back.

Monsoon June to September Prime time

Jul-Sep the falls are at full strength; the path crosses streams and the plunge pool is deep, so do not swim.

Autumn October to November Prime time

October still has good flow under clear skies, and November is cooler and quieter for the walk past the painted rock shelters.

What Lakhaniya Dari (Ahraura) is actually like

No food at the falls, so pack lunch; weekend groups from Varanasi and Mirzapur, quiet weekdays.

    Waterfall · 16 km out

    Stepped falls in Chandraprabha Wildlife Sanctuary, with Devdari a short walk away.

Common questions

When is the best time to visit Lakhaniya Dari (Ahraura)?

August to October is peak season in Lakhaniya Dari (Ahraura). July and November is shoulder season: thinner crowds, a bit more risk in the weather. Avoid April to June. Jul-Sep the falls are at full strength; the path crosses streams and the plunge pool is deep, so do not swim.

How many days do you need in Lakhaniya Dari (Ahraura)?

1 day is the usual stay. No food at the falls, so pack lunch; weekend groups from Varanasi and Mirzapur, quiet weekdays.

How do you get to Lakhaniya Dari (Ahraura)?

Ahraura town about 9 km; Varanasi about 50 km by road and its airport about 2 h; Chunar railhead about 30 km. Practical ways in: flight, train and road.

Is Lakhaniya Dari (Ahraura) expensive, and does it get crowded?

Shoestring by Indian travel standards, and it runs steady at the busiest time of year. Doable solo with some planning.

What are the best things to do in Lakhaniya Dari?

Lekhania rock shelters, Bhandari Devi Temple and Rajdari Falls. Painted rock shelters along the Lakhaniya valley, recorded by archaeologists in the 1930s; look for red ochre figures.
