Leh season by season

Winter December to February Do not go

Leh empties out below -15C, most guesthouses shut, though niche snow-leopard and Chadar trips still run.

Summer March to May Shoulder

Icebound into April, flights only; by May the Srinagar-Leh highway reopens and season begins.

Monsoon June to September Prime time

Peak season - both highways open, dry rain-shadow weather, hostels and cafes buzzing.

Autumn October to November Shoulder

October is a sweet spot: clear skies, golden poplars, thinning crowds before passes shut in November.

Signature festival

Sep: Ladakh Festival

Things to do in Leh

The local places worth your time in and around Leh, with how far each sits from the centre. Tap a name to open it in Google Maps.

  • Leh Palace

    Palace · 1 km out

    Nine storey 17th century Namgyal palace over the old town; rooms are bare, you go for the roof and the Stok range view

  • Shanti Stupa

    Heritage · 3 km out

    White dome above Changspa reached by 500 steps or a back road; sunset is the point and the stairway is lit for the walk down

  • Namgyal Tsemo Gompa

    Monastery · 2 km out

    Fort and gompa on the ridge crest above the palace with a gold Maitreya; 20 min climb, shrine opens morning and evening

  • Thiksey Monastery

    Monastery · 19 km out

    Twelve storey monastery stacked over the Indus with a 15 m Maitreya; be there by 7 am for prayers, buses run from Leh

  • Hemis Monastery

    Monastery · 45 km out

    Ladakh's wealthiest gompa hidden in a side gorge, with a serious museum; the June festival packs it, mornings are quiet

  • Central Asian Museum

    Museum · in town

    Stone caravan tower in the old town on Leh's Yarkand trade; small fee, and it pairs with the restored old town lanes

  • Leh Main Bazaar

    Market · in town

    Pedestrian bazaar street with Ladakhi women selling greens and apricots at the top end; liveliest after 6 pm

  • Khardung La

    Viewpoint · 39 km out

    The 5359 m pass over to Nubra with a cafe and an inflated height sign; permits checked at South Pullu, do not linger up top

Common questions

When is the best time to visit Leh?

June to September is peak season in Leh. May and October is shoulder season: thinner crowds, a bit more risk in the weather. Avoid November to April. Peak season - both highways open, dry rain-shadow weather, hostels and cafes buzzing.

How many days do you need in Leh?

5 days is the usual stay. Rooftop cafes full of bikers; easy to join a shared taxi to Pangong or Nubra.

How do you get to Leh?

Fly Delhi-Leh year-round; Manali/Srinagar highway Jun-Sep. Practical ways in: flight and road.

Is Leh expensive, and does it get crowded?

Mid-range by Indian travel standards, and it runs busy at the busiest time of year. Made for solo travellers.

Is there a festival in Leh?

Sep: Ladakh Festival. Time a trip around it and you see the place at its loudest, but book beds early.

What are the best things to do in Leh?

Leh Palace, Shanti Stupa, Namgyal Tsemo Gompa, Thiksey Monastery, Hemis Monastery and Central Asian Museum, and more. Nine storey 17th century Namgyal palace over the old town; rooms are bare, you go for the roof and the Stok range view.
