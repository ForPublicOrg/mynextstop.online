Shivanasamudra Falls season by season

Winter December to February Shoulder

Dec-Jan carries decent leftover flow with easy 28 °C weather; by February the curtains narrow to ribbons on the rock face.

Summer March to May Do not go

Mar-May is a trickle over bare basalt at 36 °C on the open Deccan; not worth the drive unless the dams release upstream.

Monsoon June to September Prime time

Jul-Sep is the spectacle: Gaganachukki becomes a churning wall of whitewater and the viewing rails pack out on weekends.

Autumn October to November Prime time

October is the sweet spot, full falls and washed air; November stays good, and coracles start running below Bharachukki.

What Shivanasamudra Falls is actually like

Weekend day-trippers from Bengaluru and Mysuru at the railings; sleep in Malavalli or a farmstay, not at the falls.

Things to do in Shivanasamudra Falls

The local places worth your time in and around Shivanasamudra Falls, with how far each sits from the centre. Tap a name to open it in Google Maps.

  • Barachukki Falls

    Waterfall · 11 km out

    The eastern cataract, steps down to the spray and coracles when the flow drops; 20 minutes round by road

  • Talakadu

    Heritage · 30 km out

    Shiva temples dug out of the Kaveri sand dunes, walked barefoot; go early or after 4 pm, the sand burns

  • Chennakeshava Temple, Somanathapura

    Temple · 35 km out

    The most complete of the three Hoysala star plan temples; ASI ticket, gates shut 5.30 pm, no puja inside

  • Dargah Hazrat Mardane Ghaib

    Shrine · 1 km out

    Cliff top dargah beside the Gaganachukki gorge with the falls roaring below; shoes off and heads covered

  • Shivanasamudra Power House

    Heritage · 2 km out

    Asia's first hydroelectric station, generating since 1902; a working plant, so you see it from the road only

  • Bheemeshwari

    River · 45 km out

    Kaveri camp in the Cauvery sanctuary, coracles, otters and river birds; day entry needs a call ahead

Getting to Shivanasamudra Falls

Common questions

When is the best time to visit Shivanasamudra Falls?

July to October is peak season in Shivanasamudra Falls. June and November to January is shoulder season: thinner crowds, a bit more risk in the weather. Avoid March to May. Jul-Sep is the spectacle: Gaganachukki becomes a churning wall of whitewater and the viewing rails pack out on weekends.

How many days do you need in Shivanasamudra Falls?

1 day is the usual stay. Weekend day-trippers from Bengaluru and Mysuru at the railings; sleep in Malavalli or a farmstay, not at the falls.

How do you get to Shivanasamudra Falls?

Buses from Mysuru 75 km or Bengaluru 130 km to Malavalli, then a local bus or auto 15 km to the viewpoints. Practical ways in: road.

Is Shivanasamudra Falls expensive, and does it get crowded?

Shoestring by Indian travel standards, and it runs busy at the busiest time of year. Fine solo.

What are the best things to do in Shivanasamudra Falls?

Barachukki Falls, Talakadu, Chennakeshava Temple, Somanathapura, Dargah Hazrat Mardane Ghaib, Shivanasamudra Power House and Bheemeshwari. The eastern cataract, steps down to the spray and coracles when the flow drops; 20 minutes round by road.
