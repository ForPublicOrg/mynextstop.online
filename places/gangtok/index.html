Gangtok season by season

Winter December to February Shoulder

Cold but clear: good Kanchenjunga views on good days, North Sikkim trips possible if roads hold.

Summer March to May Prime time

Mar-Apr is peak (flower festival, clearest skies); by May haze builds ahead of the monsoon.

Monsoon June to September Shoulder

Heavy rain and landslides regularly cut NH10 to the plains. The town's fine, travel isn't.

Autumn October to November Prime time

Post-monsoon clarity returns: the best combination of clear peaks and reliable road access.

Things to do in Gangtok

The local places worth your time in and around Gangtok, with how far each sits from the centre. Tap a name to open it in Google Maps.

  • Tsomgo Lake (Changu Lake)

    Lake · 38 km out

    Glacial lake at 3,750 m on the Nathu La road; permit and shared jeep booked a day ahead, and it freezes over in January.

  • Rumtek Monastery

    Monastery · 24 km out

    Seat of the Karmapa, a big Kagyu complex with a golden stupa; carry photo ID for the army post at the gate.

  • MG Marg

    Market · in town

    The pedestrian spine of town, benches and cafes, no vehicles and no smoking; where everyone ends up after dark.

  • Nathu La

    Viewpoint · 54 km out

    Old silk road pass on the China border at 4,300 m; Indian passports only, permit via an agent, shut Mondays and Tuesdays.

  • Enchey Monastery

    Monastery · 3 km out

    Nyingma gompa founded in the 1840s, up in the pines above town; a 40 minute uphill walk, quietest at morning prayers.

  • Do Drul Chorten

    Heritage · 2 km out

    Stupa ringed by 108 prayer wheels with a monastic school beside it; walk it clockwise, 20 minutes off MG Marg.

  • Namgyal Institute of Tibetology

    Museum · 3 km out

    Tibetan manuscripts, thangkas and ritual objects in a 1958 hall; closed Sundays and small enough for an hour.

  • Tashi View Point

    Viewpoint · 8 km out

    Roadside deck built for the Kanchenjunga sunrise; leave town by 4.30 am in season, the tea stall opens with the crowd.

Common questions

When is the best time to visit Gangtok?

March to April and October to November is peak season in Gangtok. May, September and December to February is shoulder season: thinner crowds, a bit more risk in the weather. Avoid June to August. Mar-Apr is peak (flower festival, clearest skies); by May haze builds ahead of the monsoon.

How many days do you need in Gangtok?

3 days is the usual stay. Zostel crowd planning North Sikkim runs; easy to share permits and jeep seats.

How do you get to Gangtok?

Fly Bagdogra, 4hr shared taxi; Indians need a free ILP. Practical ways in: flight and road.

Is Gangtok expensive, and does it get crowded?

Mid-range by Indian travel standards, and it runs busy at the busiest time of year. Easy solo.

Is there a festival in Gangtok?

May: International Flower Festival. Time a trip around it and you see the place at its loudest, but book beds early.

What are the best things to do in Gangtok?

Tsomgo Lake (Changu Lake), Rumtek Monastery, MG Marg, Nathu La, Enchey Monastery and Do Drul Chorten, and more. Glacial lake at 3,750 m on the Nathu La road; permit and shared jeep booked a day ahead, and it freezes over in January.
