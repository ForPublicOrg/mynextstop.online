Nampong (Pangsau Pass) season by season

Winter December to February Prime time

Dec-Feb are dry, 25 C days and cool nights; the January festival brings Myanmar dancers and traders over the pass, and the lake permit from the SDO is easiest then

Summer March to May Shoulder

Mar-May heat past 32 C at 300 m with storms from April; the pass at 1,160 m is a cooler day out and the Stilwell Road's WWII sites are still dry enough to reach

Monsoon June to September Shoulder

Jun-Aug rain buries the pass in cloud and the 12 km road up from Nampong slips regularly, so sit those months out; September is still wet but the road reopens

Autumn October to November Prime time

Oct-Nov clear the cloud and the pass view over the Pangsau lake basin returns, with Namdapha 60 km away for the second half of the trip and no crowds

Signature festival

20-22 Jan: Pangsau Pass International Festival at Nampong, with Myanmar traders and dancers

Common questions

When is the best time to visit Nampong (Pangsau Pass)?

November to February is peak season in Nampong (Pangsau Pass). March to May and September to October is shoulder season: thinner crowds, a bit more risk in the weather. Avoid June to August. Dec-Feb are dry, 25 C days and cool nights; the January festival brings Myanmar dancers and traders over the pass, and the lake permit from the SDO is easiest then.

How many days do you need in Nampong (Pangsau Pass)?

2 days is the usual stay. A tea-shop bazaar, forest and PWD rest houses, a lodge or two; Assam Rifles, Tangsa villagers and no one else outside festival week.

How do you get to Nampong (Pangsau Pass)?

Tinsukia to Jairampur by bus or Sumo, 2-3 h via Ledo, then 13 km on to Nampong; the pass needs an SDO permit. ILP needed. Practical ways in: flight, train and road.

Is Nampong (Pangsau Pass) expensive, and does it get crowded?

Shoestring by Indian travel standards, and it runs quiet at the busiest time of year. Doable solo with some planning.

Is there a festival in Nampong (Pangsau Pass)?

20-22 Jan: Pangsau Pass International Festival at Nampong, with Myanmar traders and dancers. Time a trip around it and you see the place at its loudest, but book beds early.
