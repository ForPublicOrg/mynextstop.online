Talasari Beach (Chandaneswar) season by season

Winter December to February Prime time

Nov-Feb is the window: 26 C days, a hard sand bar you can walk for 3 km at low tide, and creek boats running on every tide.

Summer March to May Shoulder

March is still easy going. By May the sand glares at 38 C, though the mid-April Chandaneswar mela packs the temple lane inland.

Monsoon June to September Quiet, but open

Jun-Sep the sea turns brown and choppy, boatmen skip the creek crossing on rough days and the casuarina grove stays soaked.

Autumn October to November Prime time

October clears fast once the rains stop and the Digha crowds have not arrived; a late Bay cyclone can shut the beach for a day.

Signature festival

mid-Apr: Chandaneswar mela on Chaitra Sankranti

Common questions

When is the best time to visit Talasari Beach (Chandaneswar)?

November to February is peak season in Talasari Beach (Chandaneswar). March to April and October is shoulder season: thinner crowds, a bit more risk in the weather. No month is a write-off here. October clears fast once the rains stop and the Digha crowds have not arrived; a late Bay cyclone can shut the beach for a day.

How many days do you need in Talasari Beach (Chandaneswar)?

2 days is the usual stay. Bengali day-trippers off the Digha buses by noon, a handful of beach cottages and fish-fry stalls; the sand is yours after dark.

How do you get to Talasari Beach (Chandaneswar)?

Digha railhead 12 km, or Jaleswar station 30 km; bus or auto to Chandaneswar, then 3 km on to the sand. Practical ways in: train and road.

Is Talasari Beach (Chandaneswar) expensive, and does it get crowded?

Shoestring by Indian travel standards, and it runs steady at the busiest time of year. Fine solo.

Is there a festival in Talasari Beach (Chandaneswar)?

mid-Apr: Chandaneswar mela on Chaitra Sankranti. Time a trip around it and you see the place at its loudest, but book beds early.

What are the best things to do in Talasari Beach?

Chandaneswar Temple, Jagannath Temple, Digha, Marine Aquarium and Regional Centre, Digha, Shankarpur Beach and Bichitrapur Mangrove Forest. Shiva temple near the Odisha and Bengal border; its Pana Sankranti fair in Chaitra draws about half a million.
