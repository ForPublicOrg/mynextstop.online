Kiradu Temples season by season

Winter December to February Prime time

At 9-26 C you can climb over the Someshvara temple's carved plinth in near silence; the ASI caretaker may be the only other person.

Summer March to May Shoulder

April-June runs to 45 C on bare rock with no shade and no water stall, and there is no dependable bus back to Barmer after mid-afternoon.

Monsoon June to September Mixed, read the detail

July-September brings under 300 mm so the scrub greens briefly, but 38 C with humidity makes wandering the open ruins punishing.

Autumn October to November Prime time

October-November cools fast and late-afternoon light on the sandstone is the best of the year, with almost nobody there on a weekday.

Common questions

When is the best time to visit Kiradu Temples?

November to February is peak season in Kiradu Temples. March and October is shoulder season: thinner crowds, a bit more risk in the weather. Avoid April to June. October-November cools fast and late-afternoon light on the sandstone is the best of the year, with almost nobody there on a weekday.

How many days do you need in Kiradu Temples?

1 day is the usual stay. ASI ruins with a caretaker and a village tea stall; local legend says do not linger after dark, and nobody does.

How do you get to Kiradu Temples?

Barmer railhead 35 km by road; no dependable bus, so share a jeep or hire a taxi for the half day. Practical ways in: train and road.

Is Kiradu Temples expensive, and does it get crowded?

Shoestring by Indian travel standards, and it runs quiet at the busiest time of year. Doable solo with some planning.
