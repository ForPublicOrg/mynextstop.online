Phawngpui (Blue Mountain) season by season

Winter December to February Prime time

Nov-Mar is dry at 5-20 C on top, and the Farpak meadow walk and the Thlazuang Kham cliff edge are straightforward.

Summer March to May Prime time

Rhododendron and orchids peak in March-April, but from May the Sangau road turns to clay and the summit clouds in.

Monsoon June to September Do not go

Jun-Sep the park is shut outright: permits are issued only from November to end-April, and the Sangau road slips under the rain anyway.

Autumn October to November Prime time

October is still outside the permit window: the park only reopens in November, so the summit and Farpak are off-limits.

Common questions

When is the best time to visit Phawngpui (Blue Mountain)?

November to March is peak season in Phawngpui (Blue Mountain). April is shoulder season: thinner crowds, a bit more risk in the weather. Avoid May to October. October is still outside the permit window: the park only reopens in November, so the summit and Farpak are off-limits.

How many days do you need in Phawngpui (Blue Mountain)?

3 days is the usual stay. You sleep in Sangau village homestays and walk up with a forest guard, expecting no other trekkers at all.

How do you get to Phawngpui (Blue Mountain)?

ILP for Indians; Aizawl to Sangau 230 km, 9-10 h by shared sumo, then a hired jeep to the park gate. Practical ways in: road.

Is Phawngpui (Blue Mountain) expensive, and does it get crowded?

Mid-range by Indian travel standards, and it runs quiet at the busiest time of year. Doable solo with some planning.

What are the best things to do in Phawngpui?

Farpak, Thlazuang Kham and Sangau. Grass meadow below the summit where the trail comes out, orchids and rhododendron in March; the park camp sits here.
