Mangar Bani season by season

Winter December to February Prime time

Dec-Feb gives 8-22 C mornings for the valley loop to the Gudariya Baba shrine: wintering warblers, raptors and the clearest walking light.

Summer March to May Shoulder

Only early March works; from April to June the dhau forest stands grey and leafless at 40-44 C with no shade or water on the trails.

Monsoon June to September Prime time

June is a furnace, but July-Sept rain flips the dhau canopy deep green, the one window when these Aravallis genuinely look alive.

Autumn October to November Shoulder

Walking weather returns late October and the first winter birds with it, though the green fades and NCR haze thickens through November.

Common questions

When is the best time to visit Mangar Bani?

August to September and December to February is peak season in Mangar Bani. March, July and October to November is shoulder season: thinner crowds, a bit more risk in the weather. Avoid April to June. June is a furnace, but July-Sept rain flips the dhau canopy deep green, the one window when these Aravallis genuinely look alive.

How many days do you need in Mangar Bani?

1 day is the usual stay. Dawn birders, Delhi nature-walk groups and Gujjar herders; camp at Mangar-Dhauj farm camps or sleep back in Gurugram.

How do you get to Mangar Bani?

No public transport: cab off the Gurgaon-Faridabad road, 45 min from Gurugram or Faridabad; Delhi day trip. Practical ways in: flight, train and road.

Is Mangar Bani expensive, and does it get crowded?

Shoestring by Indian travel standards, and it runs quiet at the busiest time of year. Doable solo with some planning.
