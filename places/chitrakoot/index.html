Chitrakoot (Ramghat) season by season

Winter December to February Prime time

Cool ghat mornings, evening aarti on the Mandakini and an easy 5 km Kamadgiri parikrama; weekends bring big pilgrim crowds.

Summer March to May Shoulder

Bundelkhand cooks past 44 °C from April to June; stone ghats radiate heat and the bare parikrama path is brutal by 9 am.

Monsoon June to September Mixed, read the detail

Rain fills the Mandakini and greens the hills, but ghats turn slippery and Sawan Mondays bring heavy pilgrim surges.

Autumn October to November Prime time

Best window: washed skies for Hanuman Dhara's cliff steps, and the Deepdan mela floods Ramghat with lamps at Diwali.

Signature festival

Things to do in Chitrakoot

The local places worth your time in and around Chitrakoot, with how far each sits from the centre. Tap a name to open it in Google Maps.

  • Kamadgiri

    Temple · 5 km out

    Forested hill circled by a 5 km barefoot parikrama past the Bharat Milap temple; walk it early, the paved path gets hot

  • Hanuman Dhara

    Temple · 5 km out

    Cliffside shrine up roughly 360 steps with a spring above the idol; climb at sunrise, the valley view is the reward

  • Gupt Godavari

    Cave · 18 km out

    Two dripping caves with a stream you wade ankle deep; go before noon, queues back up at the narrow mouth later

  • Sphatik Shila

    Shrine · 4 km out

    Rock by the Mandakini with footprints said to be Ram's, in quiet forest; pair it with the boat back to Ramghat

  • Sati Anusuya Ashram

    Temple · 16 km out

    Ashram in a wooded valley where the Mandakini rises; shared jeeps run from Ramghat, the last one back leaves by dusk

  • Janki Kund

    Ghat · 2 km out

    Riverside bathing spot where Sita is said to have washed; a 20 min walk along the bank from Ramghat, or a short boat ride

Common questions

When is the best time to visit Chitrakoot (Ramghat)?

November to February is peak season in Chitrakoot (Ramghat). March and October is shoulder season: thinner crowds, a bit more risk in the weather. Avoid April to June. Best window: washed skies for Hanuman Dhara's cliff steps, and the Deepdan mela floods Ramghat with lamps at Diwali.

How many days do you need in Chitrakoot (Ramghat)?

2 days is the usual stay. Pilgrim-town rhythm: dharamshalas and simple hotels near Ramghat, sadhus, boatmen and langur troops for company.

How do you get to Chitrakoot (Ramghat)?

Chitrakoot Dham Karwi station 10 km with direct Delhi trains; Prayagraj 110 km, autos run Karwi to Ramghat. Practical ways in: train and road.

Is Chitrakoot (Ramghat) expensive, and does it get crowded?

Shoestring by Indian travel standards, and it runs steady at the busiest time of year. Fine solo.

Is there a festival in Chitrakoot (Ramghat)?

Oct/Nov: Deepdan mela, lamps on the Mandakini at Diwali. Time a trip around it and you see the place at its loudest, but book beds early.

What are the best things to do in Chitrakoot?

Kamadgiri, Hanuman Dhara, Gupt Godavari, Sphatik Shila, Sati Anusuya Ashram and Janki Kund. Forested hill circled by a 5 km barefoot parikrama past the Bharat Milap temple; walk it early, the paved path gets hot.
