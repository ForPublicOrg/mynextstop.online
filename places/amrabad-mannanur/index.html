Mannanur (Amrabad Tiger Reserve) season by season

Winter December to February Prime time

Dec to Feb mornings drop near 12 C up at 620 m, jeeps run from the Mannanur gate and the Farhabad cliff over the Krishna is clear at dawn.

Summer March to May Shoulder

March is the last easy month, with animals tied to the waterholes. April sits at 42 C and May goes past 44, brutal in an open jeep.

Monsoon June to September Shoulder

Jun to Aug the forest tracks to Farhabad turn to slush and safaris stop. Late September dries them with the Nallamala at its greenest.

Autumn October to November Prime time

October reopens the tracks with the grass still shoulder high. November cools the nights and starts the best sighting run of the year.

Common questions

When is the best time to visit Mannanur (Amrabad Tiger Reserve)?

November to February is peak season in Mannanur (Amrabad Tiger Reserve). March and September to October is shoulder season: thinner crowds, a bit more risk in the weather. Avoid May to August. October reopens the tracks with the grass still shoulder high. November cools the nights and starts the best sighting run of the year.

How many days do you need in Mannanur (Amrabad Tiger Reserve)?

3 days is the usual stay. Forest department cottages and a dhaba strip on the Srisailam highway, Chenchu hamlets down the side tracks, no nightlife at all.

How do you get to Mannanur (Amrabad Tiger Reserve)?

Hyderabad to Srisailam buses stop at Mannanur, 150 km and about 4 h; Hyderabad is also the nearest railhead and airport. Practical ways in: flight, train and road.

Is Mannanur (Amrabad Tiger Reserve) expensive, and does it get crowded?

Mid-range by Indian travel standards, and it runs quiet at the busiest time of year. Doable solo with some planning.
