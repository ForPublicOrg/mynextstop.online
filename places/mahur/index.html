Mahur (Mahurgad) season by season

Winter December to February Prime time

December to February gives cold mornings on the three hills, clear air over the Painganga valley and short darshan queues on any weekday.

Summer March to May Shoulder

March is still fine if you climb early; by May the Marathwada plain touches 44 C and the stone steps are too hot to stand on until the sun drops.

Monsoon June to September Shoulder

July to September the hills go properly green and the Kinwat forest road is a pleasure, but cloud sits on the shrine and the ghat turns slippery.

Autumn October to November Prime time

Navratri in October brings the year's biggest yatra and a proper fair; November empties out and leaves the best walking between the three hilltops.

Common questions

When is the best time to visit Mahur (Mahurgad)?

October to February is peak season in Mahur (Mahurgad). March and July to September is shoulder season: thinner crowds, a bit more risk in the weather. Avoid May. December to February gives cold mornings on the three hills, clear air over the Painganga valley and short darshan queues on any weekday.

How many days do you need in Mahur (Mahurgad)?

2 days is the usual stay. Dharamshalas and plain lodges by the temple steps; pilgrims at dawn, then the hill goes quiet all afternoon.

How do you get to Mahur (Mahurgad)?

Kinwat railhead 45 km or Nanded 130 km, then a state bus up the ghat, about an hour from Kinwat. Practical ways in: train and road.

Is Mahur (Mahurgad) expensive, and does it get crowded?

Shoestring by Indian travel standards, and it runs steady at the busiest time of year. Fine solo.

Is there a festival in Mahur (Mahurgad)?

Oct: Navratri yatra climbs to the Renuka shrine. Time a trip around it and you see the place at its loudest, but book beds early.
