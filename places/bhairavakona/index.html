Bhairavakona season by season

Winter December to February Prime time

Dec-Feb is the season: 28 C days, dry rock underfoot for the scramble between all eight shrines, and cold clear nights in the gorge.

Summer March to May Shoulder

March is the last workable month. Apr-Jun bake the bare amphitheatre past 44 C, the fall is dry and there is no shade, so skip it.

Monsoon June to September Shoulder

Jul-Sep rain arrives late and thin over the Veligondas; by September the fall is running again and the last track in turns to mud.

Autumn October to November Prime time

Oct-Nov is best. The northeast monsoon fills the waterfall, the cliff ring turns green, and even weekends stay close to empty.

Feb/Mar: Maha Shivaratri fair at the cave shrines

Common questions

When is the best time to visit Bhairavakona?

October to January is peak season in Bhairavakona. February to March and July to September is shoulder season: thinner crowds, a bit more risk in the weather. Avoid April to June. Oct-Nov is best. The northeast monsoon fills the waterfall, the cliff ring turns green, and even weekends stay close to empty.

How many days do you need in Bhairavakona?

2 days is the usual stay. A stream, a temple choultry and a few pilgrim sheds; day visitors leave by five and the gorge goes quiet except for langurs.

How do you get to Bhairavakona?

Train to Nellore or Ongole, bus to Chandrasekharapuram or Podili, then 20 km of rough forest road by jeep or auto. Practical ways in: train and road.

Is Bhairavakona expensive, and does it get crowded?

Shoestring by Indian travel standards, and it runs quiet at the busiest time of year. Doable solo with some planning.

Is there a festival in Bhairavakona?

Feb/Mar: Maha Shivaratri fair at the cave shrines. Time a trip around it and you see the place at its loudest, but book beds early.
