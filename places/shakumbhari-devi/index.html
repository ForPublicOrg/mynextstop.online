Shakumbhari Devi (Behat) season by season

Winter December to February Prime time

Dec and Jan drop to 4C at night with the Shivalik ridgelines standing clear; weekdays are quiet and the forest track is dry

Summer March to May Prime time

March is the best month, green and mild after the winter rain; April warms quickly and May and June push 40C on the plains below

Monsoon June to September Shoulder

July and August the seasonal nala the approach road crosses runs hard and washes out, and the last few km are regularly cut

Autumn October to November Prime time

October and November are cool and washed clean, the riverbed walkable, and the Ashwin Navratri fair fills the valley for nine days

Signature festival

Common questions

When is the best time to visit Shakumbhari Devi (Behat)?

February to March and October to November is peak season in Shakumbhari Devi (Behat). April, September and December to January is shoulder season: thinner crowds, a bit more risk in the weather. Avoid July to August. Dec and Jan drop to 4C at night with the Shivalik ridgelines standing clear; weekdays are quiet and the forest track is dry.

How many days do you need in Shakumbhari Devi (Behat)?

2 days is the usual stay. Temple dharamshalas and a row of dhabas at the gate, nothing else; pilgrims by day, then only forest noise and bells.

How do you get to Shakumbhari Devi (Behat)?

Saharanpur is the railhead 40 km south, buses and shared jeeps run up via Behat, last one back leaves mid afternoon. Practical ways in: train and road.

Is Shakumbhari Devi (Behat) expensive, and does it get crowded?

Shoestring by Indian travel standards, and it runs steady at the busiest time of year. Doable solo with some planning.

Is there a festival in Shakumbhari Devi (Behat)?

Mar/Apr: Chaitra Navratri fair, again in Sep/Oct. Time a trip around it and you see the place at its loudest, but book beds early.
