Mandar Hill (Bounsi) season by season

Winter December to February Prime time

Dec-Feb is the season: cool mornings for the stepped climb to the Jain shrine on the summit, and the Makar Sankranti mela in mid-Jan fills Bounsi for a week

Summer March to May Shoulder

March still works at first light; by May the bare granite radiates 43 C by 10 am and the steps are a punishment, so leave it until the rains

Monsoon June to September Mixed, read the detail

June still burns; from Jul the hill greens and the Papharni tank fills round its island temple, but the steps run slick and storms build by afternoon, so go up early

Autumn October to November Prime time

Oct-Nov bring clear post-monsoon air, the plain still green below the summit ledges, and few people outside weekend pilgrim groups

Signature festival

mid-Jan: Bounsi mela at Makar Sankranti

Common questions

When is the best time to visit Mandar Hill (Bounsi)?

November to February is peak season in Mandar Hill (Bounsi). March and October is shoulder season: thinner crowds, a bit more risk in the weather. Avoid May to June. Oct-Nov bring clear post-monsoon air, the plain still green below the summit ledges, and few people outside weekend pilgrim groups.

How many days do you need in Mandar Hill (Bounsi)?

1 day is the usual stay. Pilgrims and local picnic groups by day; sleep in a basic Bounsi lodge or the Jain dharamshala, or go back to Bhagalpur 45 km away.

How do you get to Mandar Hill (Bounsi)?

Train to Mandar Hill station at Bounsi (Bhagalpur 1.5h), or bus from Bhagalpur or Deoghar; autos do the last 4 km to the hill. Practical ways in: train and road.

Is Mandar Hill (Bounsi) expensive, and does it get crowded?

Shoestring by Indian travel standards, and it runs steady at the busiest time of year. Doable solo with some planning.

Is there a festival in Mandar Hill (Bounsi)?

mid-Jan: Bounsi mela at Makar Sankranti. Time a trip around it and you see the place at its loudest, but book beds early.
