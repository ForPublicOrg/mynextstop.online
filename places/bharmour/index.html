Bharmour (Manimahesh) season by season

Winter December to February Do not go

December-February Bharmour sits under snow at 2100 m and the Chamba road ices over; buses run thin and most guesthouses shut.

Summer March to May Prime time

May-June is Gaddi season at 12-25C, flocks moving up to the passes and the Chaurasi courtyard warm enough to sit in all day.

Monsoon June to September Prime time

July is landslide month on the Chamba road, and the Aug-Sep Manimahesh yatra puts lakhs of pilgrims on a one-lane hill road.

Autumn October to November Prime time

September-October after the yatra is the best of it: clear Kailash views, empty temples and firm ground on the trek up.

Signature festival

Aug/Sep: Manimahesh Yatra, Janmashtami to Radhashtami

Common questions

When is the best time to visit Bharmour (Manimahesh)?

May to June and September to October is peak season in Bharmour (Manimahesh). April, August and November is shoulder season: thinner crowds, a bit more risk in the weather. Avoid July and December to February. May-June is Gaddi season at 12-25C, flocks moving up to the passes and the Chaurasi courtyard warm enough to sit in all day.

How many days do you need in Bharmour (Manimahesh)?

3 days is the usual stay. Shepherd families, HPTDC huts and a few guesthouses; outside yatra season you are the only non-pilgrim in the village.

How do you get to Bharmour (Manimahesh)?

65 km and 3 h by HRTC bus from Chamba; Pathankot railhead 185 km; Manimahesh trek starts at Hadsar, 13 km on. Practical ways in: road.

Is Bharmour (Manimahesh) expensive, and does it get crowded?

Shoestring by Indian travel standards, and it runs steady at the busiest time of year. Doable solo with some planning.

Is there a festival in Bharmour (Manimahesh)?

Aug/Sep: Manimahesh Yatra, Janmashtami to Radhashtami. Time a trip around it and you see the place at its loudest, but book beds early.
