Purne (Phugtal Gompa) season by season

Winter December to February Do not go

Dec-Feb the valley is sealed by snow and the monks walk the frozen Tsarap for supplies; there is no jeep and no bed for you.

Summer March to May Do not go

Mar-May the Lungnak track stays blocked, and even the Padum road rarely reopens before the end of May, so this is not the season.

Monsoon June to September Prime time

June the Lungnak jeeps restart; Jul-Sep is the season, warm days, the Tsarap grey with melt and a dry 2h walk up to the gompa.

Autumn October to November Shoulder

Early Oct is clear and empty with the barley cut; from November the jeeps stop and the valley closes for seven months.

What Purne (Phugtal Gompa) is actually like

Two homestays and a camp at the river fork; trekkers off the Shinku La trail share dal, tsampa and butter tea after dark.

Getting to Purne (Phugtal Gompa)

Reach Padum from Kargil or Manali, then a shared jeep about 5h down the Lungnak valley to Purne and a 2h walk.

Common questions

When is the best time to visit Purne (Phugtal Gompa)?

July to September is peak season in Purne (Phugtal Gompa). June and October is shoulder season: thinner crowds, a bit more risk in the weather. Avoid November to May. June the Lungnak jeeps restart; Jul-Sep is the season, warm days, the Tsarap grey with melt and a dry 2h walk up to the gompa.

How many days do you need in Purne (Phugtal Gompa)?

3 days is the usual stay. Two homestays and a camp at the river fork; trekkers off the Shinku La trail share dal, tsampa and butter tea after dark.

How do you get to Purne (Phugtal Gompa)?

Reach Padum from Kargil or Manali, then a shared jeep about 5h down the Lungnak valley to Purne and a 2h walk. Practical ways in: road.

Is Purne (Phugtal Gompa) expensive, and does it get crowded?

Shoestring by Indian travel standards, and it runs quiet at the busiest time of year. Doable solo with some planning.
