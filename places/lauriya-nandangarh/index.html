Lauriya Nandangarh season by season

Winter December to February Prime time

Nov-Feb: cool, mustard in flower around the Nandangarh mound, the polished pillar shaft catching low sun; you will likely have the whole site to yourself

Summer March to May Shoulder

March is still fine at dawn; through Apr and May the open site bakes past 42 C with no shade beyond the pillar itself, so give it a miss until October

Monsoon June to September Mixed, read the detail

June is furnace hot; Jul-Sep rains turn the mound and the Gandak country deep green and the approach lanes to mud, the site stays open, keep your rickshaw waiting

Autumn October to November Prime time

Oct-Nov: clear and mild, paddy being cut around the mounds, and Chhath in late Oct or Nov jams every train into Bihar, so book that leg well ahead

What Lauriya Nandangarh is actually like

A sugar-mill village: ASI chowkidar, buffaloes on the mound, a lodge or two in Lauriya, better beds 28 km back in Bettiah

Common questions

When is the best time to visit Lauriya Nandangarh?

November to February is peak season in Lauriya Nandangarh. March and October is shoulder season: thinner crowds, a bit more risk in the weather. Avoid May to June. Oct-Nov: clear and mild, paddy being cut around the mounds, and Chhath in late Oct or Nov jams every train into Bihar, so book that leg well ahead.

How many days do you need in Lauriya Nandangarh?

1 day is the usual stay. A sugar-mill village: ASI chowkidar, buffaloes on the mound, a lodge or two in Lauriya, better beds 28 km back in Bettiah.

How do you get to Lauriya Nandangarh?

Bettiah on the Muzaffarpur-Narkatiaganj line is the railhead, then bus or shared auto 28 km northwest, 1h; Patna 5h by road. Practical ways in: train and road.

Is Lauriya Nandangarh expensive, and does it get crowded?

Shoestring by Indian travel standards, and it runs quiet at the busiest time of year. Doable solo with some planning.
