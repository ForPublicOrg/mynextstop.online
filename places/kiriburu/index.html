Kiriburu (Saranda Forest) season by season

Winter December to February Prime time

Dec-Feb mornings drop to 8-10C on the plateau, the sunset point shows ridge after ridge of blue sal, and forest permits for the Thalkobad road are easiest to get

Summer March to May Shoulder

Mar-Apr the sal sheds and flowers, dust haze thins the views and Barbil below hits 42C; May is harsh, though 880 m keeps the nights bearable

Monsoon June to September Quiet, but open

Jun-Sep Saranda turns deep green with the Koina river in spate, but the forest tracks close, leeches rule and the mine haul roads become red mud

Autumn October to November Prime time

Oct clears the post-rain haze and the streams still run; Nov is the pick, cool 12C dawns, dry tracks to Thalkobad and Karampada, and ridges visible to the horizon

What Kiriburu (Saranda Forest) is actually like

Common questions

When is the best time to visit Kiriburu (Saranda Forest)?

November to February is peak season in Kiriburu (Saranda Forest). March and October is shoulder season: thinner crowds, a bit more risk in the weather. No month is a write-off here. Oct clears the post-rain haze and the streams still run; Nov is the pick, cool 12C dawns, dry tracks to Thalkobad and Karampada, and ridges visible to the horizon.

How many days do you need in Kiriburu (Saranda Forest)?

2 days is the usual stay. SAIL township with a guest house; evenings are the sunset ledge, a chai stall and ore trucks; Thalkobad forest rest house by permit.

How do you get to Kiriburu (Saranda Forest)?

Barbil railhead 20 km, Jan Shatabdi 7 h from Howrah; 3.5 h by road from Tatanagar via Chaibasa; Bara Jamda halt 12 km. Practical ways in: train and road.

Is Kiriburu (Saranda Forest) expensive, and does it get crowded?

Shoestring by Indian travel standards, and it runs quiet at the busiest time of year. Doable solo with some planning.

What are the best things to do in Kiriburu?

Sunset Point, Meghahatuburu, Murga Mahadev Temple and Thalkobad. Viewpoint by the SAIL guest house in the twin township of Meghahatuburu, looking out over the Saranda hills.
