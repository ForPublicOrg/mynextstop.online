Punakha season by season

Winter December to February Prime time

Mild valley winters, warmer than Thimphu, with the central monk body in residence; the Drubchen battle pageant often falls in Feb.

Summer March to May Prime time

Mar and Apr are glorious, with lilac jacaranda around the dzong; by May the valley turns hot and humid at about 1,200 m.

Monsoon June to September Shoulder

Jun to Aug are hot, humid and wet, and rafting pauses on swollen rivers; Sep greens the rice terraces before harvest.

Autumn October to November Prime time

Oct and Nov are perfect: golden paddies around Chimi Lhakhang, calm rivers for rafting and clear skies on Dochu La.

Signature festival

Punakha Drubchen and Tshechu (Feb or Mar)

What Punakha is actually like

Riverside resorts and farm homestays, red rice and ema datshi, rafting crews on the Mo Chhu and pilgrims at Chimi Lhakhang.

Things to do in Punakha

The local places worth your time in and around Punakha, with how far each sits from the centre. Tap a name to open it in Google Maps.

  • Punakha Suspension Bridge

    Walk · 1 km out

    Long prayer-flag footbridge over the Pho Chhu just upstream of the dzong, swaying gently above the water.

  • Chimi Lhakhang

    Temple · 6 km out

    Fertility temple of the Divine Madman, Drukpa Kunley, built in 1499; a short walk through rice fields from Sopsokha.

  • Khamsum Yulley Namgyal Chorten

    Temple · 8 km out

    Temple-chorten built by Queen Mother Ashi Tshering Yangdon, reached by a 45 min climb through paddies with views up the Mo Chhu.

  • Mo Chhu

    River · 1 km out

    Gentle rafting stretch that finishes beside the dzong; trips run in the dry months when the river is calm.

  • Wangdue Phodrang Dzong

    Fort · 12 km out

    Ridge-top dzong of 1638, rebuilt after the 2012 fire and reconsecrated in 2022, above the Punatsang Chhu.

  • Dochu La

    Viewpoint · 15 km out

    The 108 chortens at about 3,100 m on the way in from Thimphu; stop for tea and the northern skyline.

Common questions

When is the best time to visit Punakha?

February to April and October to November is peak season in Punakha. May, September and December to January is shoulder season: thinner crowds, a bit more risk in the weather. Avoid June to August. Mild valley winters, warmer than Thimphu, with the central monk body in residence; the Drubchen battle pageant often falls in Feb.

How many days do you need in Punakha?

2 days is the usual stay. Riverside resorts and farm homestays, red rice and ema datshi, rafting crews on the Mo Chhu and pilgrims at Chimi Lhakhang.

How do you get to Punakha?

71 km from Thimphu over Dochu La, about 3 h; Paro airport is 130 km, 4 to 5 h. Route permit needed. Practical ways in: road.

Is Punakha expensive, and does it get crowded?

Mid-range by Indian travel standards, and it runs steady at the busiest time of year. Easy solo.

Is there a festival in Punakha?

Punakha Drubchen and Tshechu (Feb or Mar). Time a trip around it and you see the place at its loudest, but book beds early.

What are the best things to do in Punakha?

Punakha Suspension Bridge, Chimi Lhakhang, Khamsum Yulley Namgyal Chorten, Mo Chhu, Wangdue Phodrang Dzong and Dochu La. Long prayer-flag footbridge over the Pho Chhu just upstream of the dzong, swaying gently above the water.
