Guruvayur season by season

Winter December to February Prime time

Dec-Feb is dry at 24-32°C and the wedding season peaks: queues form before dawn and the elephants stand at Punnathur Kotta.

Summer March to May Shoulder

Mar-May is 35°C and airless, though the ten-day Utsavam in Feb-Mar is the last big draw before the heat properly sets in.

Monsoon June to September Mixed, read the detail

June-Aug is heavy rain and the queue shuffles under dripping sheds; Chavakkad beach 4 km away is unswimmable anyway.

Autumn October to November Prime time

Oct still rains most afternoons; November turns dry and cool and builds towards Ekadasi, the temple's single biggest day.

Signature festival

Nov/Dec: Guruvayur Ekadasi

Things to do in Guruvayur

The local places worth your time in and around Guruvayur, with how far each sits from the centre. Tap a name to open it in Google Maps.

  • Guruvayur Sree Krishna Temple

    Temple · in town

    The Krishna shrine the whole town exists for; nirmalyam darshan starts at 3 am, mundu or sari only, no phones, Hindus only.

  • Punnathur Kotta

    Wildlife · 4 km out

    Devaswom elephant yard inside a ruined chieftain's fort, dozens of temple elephants; small ticket at the gate, mornings are bath time.

  • Mammiyoor Mahadeva Temple

    Temple · 1 km out

    The Shiva temple pilgrims pair with Guruvayur; locals say the darshan is incomplete without it, five minutes on foot west.

  • Palayur St Thomas Church

    Church · 3 km out

    One of the seven churches St Thomas is held to have founded in 52 AD; quiet on weekday mornings, small museum by the pond.

  • Chavakkad Beach

    Beach · 6 km out

    Where Guruvayur empties out at sunset, wide grey sand at the harbour mouth; no shacks, tea and fried fish come off carts.

  • Thriprayar Sree Ramaswami Temple

    Temple · 22 km out

    Old Rama temple on the Karuvannur river, first stop of the Nalambalam circuit; get there before 11 am, then it shuts.

  • Snehatheeram Beach

    Beach · 20 km out

    Groomed DTPC beach south along the coast road, casuarina shade and a boat park; entry ticket, packed on Sunday evenings.

Common questions

When is the best time to visit Guruvayur?

November to February is peak season in Guruvayur. March and October is shoulder season: thinner crowds, a bit more risk in the weather. Avoid June to July. Oct still rains most afternoons; November turns dry and cool and builds towards Ekadasi, the temple's single biggest day.

How many days do you need in Guruvayur?

1 day is the usual stay. Pure pilgrim town: dawn queues, brass-lamp shops, cheap devaswom lodges and a strict dress code, with no other scene.

How do you get to Guruvayur?

Guruvayur has its own station off the Thrissur line; note that non-Hindus are not admitted inside the temple. Practical ways in: flight, train and road.

Is Guruvayur expensive, and does it get crowded?

Shoestring by Indian travel standards, and it runs busy at the busiest time of year. Fine solo.

Is there a festival in Guruvayur?

Nov/Dec: Guruvayur Ekadasi. Time a trip around it and you see the place at its loudest, but book beds early.

What are the best things to do in Guruvayur?

Guruvayur Sree Krishna Temple, Punnathur Kotta, Mammiyoor Mahadeva Temple, Palayur St Thomas Church, Chavakkad Beach and Thriprayar Sree Ramaswami Temple, and more. The Krishna shrine the whole town exists for; nirmalyam darshan starts at 3 am, mundu or sari only, no phones, Hindus only.
