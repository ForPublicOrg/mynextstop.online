Tamdil Lake (Saitual) season by season

Winter December to February Prime time

Dec-Feb the lake sits under a dawn mist that burns off by 9, days run 8-20 C and the lodge is mostly empty on weekdays; Aizawl families fill the picnic sheds on Saturdays.

Summer March to May Prime time

March is cool and clear enough for the forest trails; April and May turn hazy and humid, though at 780 m the lake stays cooler than Aizawl and the boats still run.

Monsoon June to September Shoulder

Jun-Sep the hills are their greenest and the lake brims, but 2,000 mm of rain means boats stop in downpours and forest paths are leechy; the Saitual road stays open.

Autumn October to November Prime time

October's washed skies make it the best month for kayaking and forest walks; November evenings turn cold at the lodge, so carry a layer for the lakeside verandah.

Common questions

When is the best time to visit Tamdil Lake (Saitual)?

October to March is peak season in Tamdil Lake (Saitual). April to May and September is shoulder season: thinner crowds, a bit more risk in the weather. No month is a write-off here. October's washed skies make it the best month for kayaking and forest walks; November evenings turn cold at the lodge, so carry a layer for the lakeside verandah.

How many days do you need in Tamdil Lake (Saitual)?

2 days is the usual stay. Weekend picnic families from Aizawl by day, a near-empty Tourism Dept lodge by night; evenings are a lakeside walk and an early dinner.

How do you get to Tamdil Lake (Saitual)?

Aizawl to Saitual about 100 km by sumo (3 h, Mon-Sat), then 6 km by taxi to the lake; Lengpui airport is 30 km beyond Aizawl. Practical ways in: flight and road.

Is Tamdil Lake (Saitual) expensive, and does it get crowded?
