Best places to visit in India in September

76 of 589 curated destinations are in peak season in September, and 204 are ones to stay away from. No listicle lies: every verdict here comes from the place's own season window.

Where India is at its best in September

Each line below is the reason this place works in the monsoon, written for that place, not a generic blurb.

  • Churdhar (via Nohradhar) Himachal Pradesh

    Sirmaur's 3,647 m Shiva summit above a sea of foothills

    June holds dry; July-August is a slippery viewless slog on clay tracks, and September clears into a superb climbing window.

    TrekSpiritualMountains

  • Doodhpathri Jammu and Kashmir

    Milky Shaliganga meadows under pine, Kashmir's gentler Gulmarg

    Jul-Aug is green and warm rather than wet, but weekend picnic crowds and pony touts peak; Jun and Sep are the quiet wins.

    MountainsOffbeat

  • Leh Ladakh

    Ladakh's backpacker capital and high-pass road-trip hub

    Peak season - both highways open, dry rain-shadow weather, hostels and cafes buzzing.

    MountainsBackpackerCulture

  • Shivanasamudra Falls Karnataka

    Twin Kaveri cataracts in full thunder after the rains

    Jul-Sep is the spectacle: Gaganachukki becomes a churning wall of whitewater and the viewing rails pack out on weekends.

    Waterfalls

  • Ninai Falls (Dediapada) Gujarat

    Monsoon cascade in the teak forests behind the Statue of Unity

    Aug-Sep is full flow: a broad white curtain off the basalt lip with mist in the canopy; Jul depends on how early the rains land.

    WaterfallsOffbeat

  • Igatpuri Maharashtra

    Vipassana meditation hub and monsoon trekking base

    Peak season for trekkers - Kalsubai and the ghats turn lush, though trails get slippery.

    MountainsSpiritualTrek

  • Ananthagiri Hills (Vikarabad) Telangana

    Deccan forest ridge where the Musi river starts, 90 km out

    Jul-Sep is when this place earns the drive, the Musi headwaters running and the whole ridge properly dense.

    MountainsTrek

  • Badrinath & Mana Uttarakhand

    Char Dham shrine at 3100 m with Mana village 3 km on

    July-August the Alaknanda valley slides; the highway above Joshimath closes for hours or days and the yatra thins right out.

    SpiritualMountainsHeritage

  • Dassam Falls Jharkhand

    The Kanchi river's clean 44 m curtain drop near Taimara village

    Jul-Sep the 44 m curtain is thunderous and strictly no-swim water: drownings happen most years, stay behind the railings.

    WaterfallsOffbeat

  • Kanthalloor Kerala

    Rain-shadow orchard village of apples, dolmens and sandalwood

    The rain shadow keeps Jun-Sep to drizzle while the rest of Kerala floods; apples ripen mid-monsoon and the valley glows green.

    OffbeatMountainsCulture

  • Banswara (Mahi Backwaters) Rajasthan

    Hundred islands of the Mahi backwaters in tribal Vagad

    Jul-Sep the wettest district in Rajasthan turns properly green, and by August the island-dotted Mahi spread is at its widest.

    OffbeatLakes

  • Kakolat Falls Bihar

    Forest waterfall with a cold bathing kund under Nawada's hills

    Jul-Aug it roars but monsoon spate shut the site for six weeks in 2025; September keeps the volume as the picnic crush eases.

    WaterfallsOffbeat

  • Chitrakote Falls (Bastar) Chhattisgarh

    India's widest waterfall in the heart of tribal Bastar

    Chitrakote's real fame: Indravati floods the 300m ledge into a genuine roar; roads turn muddy though.

    WaterfallsOffbeatCulture

  • Chorla Ghat Goa

    Cloud-forest edge of Goa above the Vazra Sakla waterfall

    Jul-Sep is the point: Vazra Sakla thunders across the valley, moss and mist everywhere; expect rain most hours and pack for it.

    MountainsWaterfallsOffbeat

  • Mangar Bani Haryana

    Sacred Gujjar grove guarding NCR's last primary Aravalli forest

    June is a furnace, but July-Sept rain flips the dhau canopy deep green, the one window when these Aravallis genuinely look alive.

    OffbeatWildlife

  • Mandu Madhya Pradesh

    Ruined Malwa plateau city famous for its monsoon mist

    Mandu's real season: mist over Roopmati's pavilion, tanks fill, roads can get slick though.

    HeritageOffbeatCulture

  • Dzukou Valley Nagaland

    Rolling green trekking valley famous for summer lilies

    The famous Dzukou lilies bloom in June-July, but you'll fight mud, leeches and fog for it.

    TrekMountainsOffbeat

  • Kodaikanal Tamil Nadu

    Misty Palani lake town loved by trekkers, backpackers

    Heavy rain and near-constant mist, Ghat road landslide risk, most viewpoints socked in - skip it

    MountainsLakesTrekBackpacker

  • Kaza (Spiti) Himachal Pradesh

    Spiti's cold-desert capital - monasteries and moonscapes

    Spiti's real peak - a rain-shadow desert, so this is HP's warmest, driest, greenest window.

    MountainsOffbeatDesertCulture

  • Katra (Vaishno Devi) Jammu and Kashmir

    Base town for the year-round Vaishno Devi pilgrimage climb

    Worst window - brutal heat plus monsoon rain that can trigger landslides and track closures.

    SpiritualTrekHeritage

  • Markha Valley Ladakh

    Ladakh's classic homestay trek below Kang Yatse's white pyramid

    Jul-Sep is prime: rain-shadow dry trails, parachute-tent cafes up, and a homestay bed with dinner waiting in every hamlet.

    TrekOffbeatMountains

  • Agumbe Karnataka

    Rainforest village known as South India's Cherrapunji

    This is the season - 700cm+ of rain, roaring falls, king cobras out, but leeches everywhere.

    OffbeatWaterfallsWildlife

  • Polo Forest (Vijaynagar) Gujarat

    Jain and Shiva ruins lost in Aravalli forest by the Harnav

    Jul-Aug is dramatic, but the river crossings towards the Sharneshwar temple flood and forest tracks turn to mud.

    OffbeatHeritageTrek

  • Gaganbawda Maharashtra

    Kolhapur's rain-drenched ghat edge above the Konkan drop

    Jul-Sep is the show: the wettest corner of Kolhapur district, every cliff runs white and the Bhuibawada ghat drives through cloud.

    OffbeatMountainsWaterfalls

  • Bogatha Falls Telangana

    Broad tiered curtain falls deep in Godavari tribal country

    Jul-Aug the falls turn into a brown thunderhead, bathing is off, and Godavari backwater can cut the Eturnagaram road.

    WaterfallsOffbeat

  • Kedarnath Uttarakhand

    Himalayan Shiva shrine at 3583m, open just 6 months

    Technically open but this is landslide and flash-flood country. The 2013 disaster hit in this window

    SpiritualTrekMountains

  • Hundru Falls Jharkhand

    Subarnarekha drops 98 m of basalt, 740 steps below the road

    Jul-Sep it is at full roar and genuinely dangerous - people drown here most years, so stay off the wet rock.

    WaterfallsTrekOffbeat

  • Athirappilly Falls Kerala

    An 80ft waterfall curtain at its thundering best in monsoon

    This is peak season here - the falls roar at full width; stick to viewing platforms, rocks are lethal

    WaterfallsTrek

  • Menal (Falls & Temples) Rajasthan

    Monsoon waterfall plunging into a gorge by 11th century temples

    Jul-Sep is the show: the Menali river leaps off the plateau in full spate with spray drifting over the shikharas; wet rock, so mind the rim.

    WaterfallsHeritageOffbeat

  • Narkanda & Thanedar Himachal Pradesh

    Apple country ridge with Hatu Peak and a one-slope ski hill

    July-August is when NH-5 slides between Shimla and Rampur; the orchards are green and the view is cloud, all day long.

    MountainsSnowOffbeat

  • Kokernag & Verinag Jammu and Kashmir

    Verinag's Mughal spring tank and Kokernag's trout gardens

    Jun-Aug is Kashmir's warm stretch near 30 C by noon with weekend picnic crowds; Sep cools down and empties out again.

    OffbeatHeritageCulture

  • Alchi (Sham Valley) Ladakh

    11th-century Kashmiri murals in a low Indus orchard village

    Jun-Sep stays dry with apricots ripening in July; the Choskor murals are unlit, so come when light reaches the doors.

    HeritageSpiritualOffbeat

  • Gokak Falls Karnataka

    Horseshoe cataract with a century-old hanging bridge at its crest

    Jul-Sep is the event: a curtain nearly 170 m wide in full roar; the hanging bridge often shuts at peak flow, so you watch from the banks

    WaterfallsHeritageOffbeat

  • Tarnetar Gujarat

    Trinetreshwar temple and Saurashtra's monsoon marriage fair

    This is the whole point: for three days in Bhadarvi, late Aug or early Sep, the fields fill with embroidered umbrellas.

    CultureOffbeatSpiritual

  • Kaas Plateau Maharashtra

    Plateau carpeted in wildflowers for three weeks a year

    Greenery builds through the rains, exploding into wildflowers by September - entry is permit-limited.

    OffbeatTrek

  • Kuntala Falls Telangana

    Telangana's tallest fall, 45 m of the Kadam off a ledge

    Jul-Sep is peak flow and the roar is the whole point, but the lower deck shuts when the river is in spate.

Common questions

Where should you travel in India in September?

76 of the 589 destinations in this catalogue are in peak season in September, with another 214 in shoulder season. The states with the most places at their best are Himachal Pradesh (16), Ladakh (14), Jammu and Kashmir (14) and Karnataka (5).

Where should you avoid in India in September?

204 places in this catalogue are marked avoid in September: closed park gates, snowed-in passes, cancelled ferries, landslide roads or plain 45 degree heat. Agartala & Unakoti, Agatti Island (Lakshadweep), Ahmedabad, Aizawl, Alibaug and Almora are among them.

Is September a good time to visit India?

September falls in the monsoon season. It is a narrower month, roughly 13 per cent of the catalogue is at its best, so it pays to pick the region carefully rather than the country.
